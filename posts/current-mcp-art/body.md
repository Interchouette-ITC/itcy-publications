A critical vulnerability has emerged in the open-source AI agent platform, exposing a dangerous flaw in how AI orchestration platforms manage security. The issue stems from an unauthenticated Model Context Protocol (MCP) bridge that allows attackers to hijack AI agents, execute arbitrary code, and access sensitive data like LLM API keys and user conversations. This flaw underscores a broader challenge in securing AI orchestration platforms - where the power to control AI agents and their interactions with enterprise systems can become a gateway for exploitation.

The risk extends beyond breaking into systems; it’s about gaining control over how AI agents behave and what they remember. Attackers could manipulate AI responses, steal credentials, and even deploy persistent AI agent swarms. As AI adoption grows, so must our focus on securing the infrastructure that powers it. Builders and security teams must now rethink how they deploy, manage, and monitor AI platforms to prevent such vulnerabilities from becoming a reality.
